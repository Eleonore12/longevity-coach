## 1. Zones

Use a 5-zone model. Always give **three anchors** for each zone: bpm, RPE, and a feel or talk-test cue. Estimated heart rates can be off by 10–15 bpm, so the felt cues serve as a reality check.

**Max HR**
- Use the measured value if the user has one (a recent race, a field test, or a watch that has seen true max efforts).
- Otherwise estimate it with Tanaka: `208 − 0.7 × age`. Label it as an estimate.

**Zone calculation**
- **Resting HR known → Karvonen (heart-rate reserve).** `HRR = maxHR − restHR`, and `zone bpm = restHR + % × HRR`.
- **Resting HR unknown → % of max HR.**

| Zone | %HRR (Karvonen) | %HRmax | RPE | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 | 50–60% | 55–65% | 2–3 | Very easy, could chat for hours. Recovery and warm-ups. |
| Z2 | 60–70% | 65–75% | 3–4 | Steady and comfortable. Full sentences possible, nasal breathing mostly possible, could hold it for 1h+. The aerobic and mitochondrial base. |
| Z3 | 70–80% | 75–85% | 5–6 | "Comfortably hard". Talking in short phrases. Use sparingly: more fatigue for less specific benefit. |
| Z4 | 80–90% | 85–92% | 7–8 | Hard. A few words at a time. Threshold work. |
| Z5 | 90–100% | 92–100% | 9–10 | Very hard to max. Can't talk. VO2max intervals and sprints. |

**If HR is distorted:** with medication that blunts heart rate, don't use bpm at all. Program with RPE and the talk test only. This covers beta-blockers and the rate-lowering calcium-channel blockers (verapamil, diltiazem). Other blood-pressure drugs, such as amlodipine, ACE inhibitors and ARBs, barely change heart rate, so bpm zones still work. Keep RPE as the main anchor anyway.

## 2. Weekly balance targets

These are adapted from mainstream longevity and exercise-physiology consensus (polarised endurance plus strength plus mobility). Scale them to the user's available time and level. Consistency matters more than hitting every target.

| Component | Beginner | Intermediate | Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zone 2 (Z1–Z2) | 60–120 min | 120–180 min | 180+ min |
| VO2max (Z5 intervals) | 0 (first 4–8 wks), then 1× | 1× | 1–2× |
| Other intensity (HIIT, Z4 tempo, sprints) | 0 | 0–1× | 1× |
| Strength | 2× full-body | 2–3× | 3–4× |
| Mobility / yoga / flow | 2–3× (10+ min each, can be short add-ons) | 3–5× | 3–5× |
| Well-being (sauna, cold, breathwork, meditation) | 1–3 touchpoints | 2–4 | 2–5 |

Additional guidance:
- **Intensity distribution:** roughly 80% of cardio time in Z1–Z2 and 20% in Z4–Z5. Keep Z3 to a minimum.
- **Stability and balance:** include some single-leg work, carries, and core anti-rotation every week. These matter more and more with age.
- **Limited time (2–3 sessions per week):** prioritise 2 full-body strength sessions plus 1 zone 2 session. Fold mobility into the warm-ups. VO2max work can be a short finisher (e.g. 4×2′ after zone 2).
- **Lots of time:** add zone 2 volume first, not more intensity.

## 3. Readiness rules

Readiness is checked at each check-in on three 1–5 scales: energy, body freshness and sleep. On all three, 5 is the good end, so body 5 means no soreness and body 1 means very sore.

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| All ≥ 3 | Proceed as planned |
| One score = 2, or high RPE yesterday | Keep the session, cut the hard part (e.g. Z4 → Z2, or −1 set), or swap the order of the week |
| Two or more scores ≤ 2, or poor sleep for 2+ nights | Recovery day: Z1 walk, mobility, yoga nidra or NSDR, breathwork |
| Symptoms **above the neck** only (mild cold) | Easy Z1–Z2 and mobility at most, nothing hard |
| Symptoms **below the neck** (fever, chest congestion, body aches, GI) | Rest. Resume progressively after 24–48h without symptoms. |
| New sharp or joint pain | Avoid loading that area. Suggest professional assessment if it persists more than a few days. |

The week is flexible. If the user skips a session, don't stack two sessions to "catch up". Just re-balance the rest of the week.

## 4. Progression tracks

Advance **one** variable at a time, and only when the previous step felt manageable (RPE as intended, good readiness). Record the current step in "Program state".

Adjusting from the last session's RPE:
- **At or below the target RPE:** advance next time.
- **1 point above target:** repeat the same step.
- **2+ points above target, or two sessions in a row above target:** step back one level.
- **Deload week:** stays at the current step with reduced volume. Advancing resumes after the deload.

**Zone 2**
- Beginner: 20–30 min sessions. Increase total weekly time by about 10% per week, or add 5–10 min to one session.
- Build up to 45–60 min sessions. Advanced users can do one long session of 75–120 min per week.

**VO2max** (only after 4–8 weeks of consistent base for beginners)
1. 6–8 × 1′ hard (Z5) / 1′ easy
2. 5 × 2′ / 2′
3. 4 × 3′ / 3′
4. 4 × 4′ / 3′ (Norwegian 4×4, the reference protocol)
5. Advanced variations: 5 × 4′, 3 × 6′ / 3′, or 30/30s × 2 sets of 10

Every VO2max session gets a 10–15 min warm-up in Z1–Z2 with 2–3 short pick-ups, and a 5–10 min cool-down. Prefer low-impact modalities (bike, rower, ski-erg, incline treadmill, uphill running, swimming) depending on injuries and access.

**HIIT / sprints** (intermediate and above, max 1× per week, and not in the same 48h window as VO2max)
- Short efforts of 10–30 s near max with full recovery (1:4 or more), 6–10 reps. Or a mixed-modal circuit at a hard effort.
- Keep the total of hard sessions (VO2max + HIIT + Z4) to 2 per week at most, unless the user is advanced.

**Strength**
- Base the program on movement patterns: squat, hinge, push, pull, carry, lunge or single-leg, core (anti-rotation and anti-extension).
- **Beginner:** 2× full-body. 1–3 sets × 8–12 reps, RPE 6–7 (stop with 3–4 reps in reserve). Learn technique first, using bodyweight or light dumbbells.
- **Intermediate:** 2–3× per week, full-body or upper/lower split. 3 sets × 6–12 reps, RPE 7–8.
- **Advanced:** 3–4× per week. Include heavier work (3–6 reps) on the main lifts, plus power (jumps, throws) if there's no joint contraindication.
- **Progression:** double progression. Add reps within the range, then add load and drop back to the bottom of the range.
- **Longevity accents:** grip (carries, hangs), single-leg stability, posterior chain, and for 40+ (especially women around perimenopause and menopause) heavy-ish lifting and some impact or jumping if tolerated, for bone density.

**Mobility, yoga and flow**
- Rotate the focus across hips, thoracic spine, shoulders, ankles, and full-body flow.
- Progress from passive stretching to active mobility (CARs, end-range strength) to flows (animal flow, vinyasa).

## 5. Blocks & deloads

- Work in 4-week blocks: weeks 1–3 progressive, week 4 deload. During a deload, cut volume by about 40%, keep some intensity but fewer intervals and sets, and add more mobility and well-being.
- Phase transitions:
  - `base` → `build` after 6–8 consistent weeks.
  - `build` → `maintain` when the user is time-limited or happy with their level.
- If the user missed most of a week (illness, travel), repeat the previous week's step instead of advancing.

## 6. Placing well-being practices

**Sauna**
- Post-workout or on rest days.
- 2–4 sessions per week is where the observational longevity data looks strongest.
- Typical session: 15–20 min at 80–100 °C, or shorter if new to it. Hydrate.
- Avoid right before a hard session.

**Cold plunge or cold shower**
- Keep it away from strength sessions: wait at least 4–6 h after, or do it on a separate day. Cold right after lifting can blunt muscle adaptations.
- Fine after zone 2, on rest days, or in the morning for alertness.
- Typical dose: 1–3 min at 10–15 °C, or a 30–60 s cold shower finish for beginners.

**Contrast (sauna, then cold)**
- Fine on recovery or zone 2 days.
- End on cold for alertness, or on warm before bed.

**Breathwork**
- Box breathing or physiological sighs for stress.
- Slow nasal breathing at about 6 breaths/min for parasympathetic down-regulation, especially in the evening.
- No hyperventilation techniques (e.g. Wim Hof style) in water or while driving, ever.

**Meditation and NSDR**
- 10–20 min. Ideal on recovery days, after training, or before sleep.

**Walks and daylight**
- Morning light plus an easy walk is a great low-cost add-on, and counts as Z1.

**Sleep**
- When readiness shows poor sleep, add one practical tip, such as caffeine timing or an evening wind-down.

## 7. Special situations

- **Travel / no equipment:** bodyweight strength (tempo squats, split squats, push-up progressions, backpack rows), hotel-room mobility flows, Z2 as brisk walking or jogging.
- **Short on time (≤ 20 min):** a strength "minimum dose" (e.g. 3 rounds of squat, push and hinge), a Z2 commute or walk, or a 4×2′ VO2 finisher. Something beats nothing.
- **Menstrual cycle:** if the user mentions symptoms, adapt to how they feel (lower intensity, more mobility) without being prescriptive. Evidence for strict cycle-phase training is mixed.
- **Pregnancy or postpartum:** no new intense training, sauna, or cold exposure. Defer to their healthcare provider (see `safety.md`).
- **Older adults (65+):** emphasise balance, power (fast concentric), strength, and fall prevention. Progress more conservatively.
